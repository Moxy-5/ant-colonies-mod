# Round 5b, reader Q: trouble at the colony, and the seam with the raids

Read at commit `a7c0a9f0` in `C:\Ant Mod` (branch `claude/motion-glue`). No code changed, nothing run or measured.

Short paths: `savanna/...` is `savanna/src/main/java/com/antcolonies/savanna/...`, `framework/...` is `framework/src/main/java/com/antcolonies/...`. "MC" is Minecraft's own 1.21.1 source as unpacked for this review.

Words used below. A **trouble** is the termite alarm's own word for one alarmed place (a break, a foe, a stamping player): it has a strength that fades (16 for a foe or a break, losing 0.2 a second, so 80 seconds from full to nothing) and it is felt as many blocks out as it is strong. A **body** is a termite that is a real mob in the world; a **number** is a termite that lives only as a count in the colony's books. A colony is **here** when a player is near enough for its insects to be bodies, and **away** when it lives as numbers only. A **goal** is one thing a mob can be doing (flee, mend, feed); Minecraft lets a mob run one moving goal at a time.

The one question followed: when something attacks or disturbs a termite colony, does every part react once, agree with the others, and go back to normal? Short answer: each part by itself is careful and ends by itself, and nothing found walls anything in or loses a termite. What does not hold is the agreeing. "Is a foe near", "is this termite safe", "is the trouble over" and "how many soldiers answer" are each judged in several places with different distances and clocks, and the two sides of a raid (the Matabele's `Raid` and the termites' `Alarm`, `RaidDefence`, danger memory) share no record of "this raid, at this place, now over".

## Findings

## Q-1 (A, proven as code; its meeting with BUGS 361 is likely): a termite alarm never ends while one living enemy insect stays within 4 blocks of it, and it walks along with that enemy

- **What is wrong.** Once a second the alarm looks at every enemy insect round the colony. An enemy within 4 blocks of a trouble (one made by a foe or a break) fills that trouble up to full strength again, and when the trouble is a foe's it also moves the trouble to where the enemy now stands. Nothing limits how long or how far. Two things follow. First, an enemy that cannot leave (the raiders stuck in termite holes of BUGS 361, 13 at once in the soak; a raider with legs cut off and left behind) holds a 16-block trouble at full strength for as long as it lives. Second, any enemy that walks slower than 4 blocks a second carries the trouble with it: a raid column going home at 2.5 blocks a second drags a 16-block alarm across the termites' land until it leaves the area the alarm looks at (64 blocks from the mound's middle for a grown colony).
- **What the player would see.** After a raid has gone home, the termites round that feeding spot do not go back to work: no worker comes out of a hole within 16 blocks, no food is fetched there, the soldiers stand at their places, and the colony keeps those soldiers as bodies even when the player is not watching from near. It lasts until the stuck ant dies or gets out. Along the raid's way home the alarm goes with the column, so termites along that way are kept in as it passes.
- **Where.** `savanna/colony/Alarm.java`: `tick` lines 354 to 368 (line 362: within `FOE_AT_TROUBLE`, 4 blocks, of any trouble that is not footsteps), `trouble` 233 to 234 (strength back up) and 246 to 248 (a foe's trouble moves to the newest place), `keepsIn` 441 to 443. `framework/entity/AbstractAntEntity.java` `mayGoOut` line 2234. `savanna/colony/Soldiers.java` `wantReal` 577 to 584 (a trouble keeps soldiers as bodies unwatched). `savanna/colony/RaidDefence.java` `tick` line 211 to 212: the seal already has the cap the alarm lacks, with the comment "(B7: a foe stuck by the break for ever no longer keeps it shut for ever)".
- **The proof.** `Alarm.isEnemy` (line 400) is `Fights.isFoe`, true for any living insect of another species that has a colony and is not paralysed (`framework/fight/Fights.java` 55 to 70). Line 362 has no count and no clock. For the walking: at one look the trouble is put where enemy X stands; a second later X is at most its speed away, so under 4 blocks a second X is still "at the trouble" and the trouble is put where X stands now. The area looked at is `foragingRadius() + 8` (`Alarm.tick` 348); the radius is the gallery web's range, reach 50 plus food radius 6 (`GalleryWeb.range` 361 to 364, `macrotermes.json` lines 145 to 149).
- **Unchecked link.** That the stuck raiders of BUGS 361 sit within 4 blocks of a trouble still alive is not measured (likely: they are stuck in the gallery they fought in). How long a stuck Matabele lives was not read. Where the soldiers that heard a walking trouble go as it moves away over ground with no gallery under it was not walked (`Alarm.cellsNear` 626 to 704). Whether a Matabele nest can lie inside the 64 blocks (then the trouble would park at the nest's door for good) depends on where new land puts them; not read.
- **No test.** `TermiteAlarmTests` checks that the alarm is calm after the foe is gone; I found no test with a foe that stays.
- **The fix proposed.** One rule "this raid is over for the termites", read by both the seal and the alarm, in place of two clocks (the seal's `lastFoe` and `SEAL_MOST`; the alarm's fade). Simplest form that removes rules instead of adding: only a foe the termites can still feel at their own cells keeps a trouble up (within 4 blocks of a gallery or nest cell, or of a termite body), a trouble never moves farther than its own reach from where it began, and it may be kept up no longer than the seal's own longest. This does not mend BUGS 361 (the raiders still cannot get out), but 361 stops freezing the termites as well.
- **Owner's call:** today termites stay shut in as long as an enemy is alive close by, which is right for a real threat at the door. My pick: keep that for an enemy at their galleries, and stop it for one walking away over open ground or shut in a pocket.

## Q-2 (A, proven): workers in a raided feeding gallery run only as far as the next roofed cell, because three rules disagree on what "safe inside" means

- **What is wrong.** Three places judge whether a termite is safe. The hunters ask "is it sheltered" (in its nest, not in a feeding gallery or runway, and no opening near): by that rule a termite in a feeding gallery is fair prey. The worker's own flight (`FleeGoal`) and its walk home (`GoInsideGoal`) ask the nest "is this cell inside", and the nest counts every roofed feeding gallery cell and every closed runway as inside. So a worker in the very gallery the ants are raiding is told "you are already inside": it does not flee, and one whose roof was just bitten off flees only to a cell that still has a roof, which can be the very next one and is still in the hunters' reach. While the alarm is on it may even keep feeding from inside (`ClipGrassGoal` line 82). The only rule that sends workers deeper is `RetreatGoal`, and it calls only the workers within about 4.5 blocks of a break, and only when that break is within 2.5 blocks of a tunnel.
- **What the player would see.** At a raided feeding spot the termite workers do not pour back down the gallery. They stay in the roofed part beside the break, some still feeding, and are stung and carried off one by one as the ants walk in. Real Macrotermes workers run down the gallery while the soldiers hold the opening. The comment on the code that brings feeders out for a watched raid says "they feed, feel the raiders and flee" (`Raid.java` 1791 to 1792); the flee part does not happen.
- **Where.** `framework/entity/goal/FleeGoal.java` `canUse` line 67 and the place to run to, line 101; `framework/entity/goal/GoInsideGoal.java` line 58; `framework/colony/Nest.java` `isInside` 1038 to 1088 (roofed gallery cells stay in the set, lines 1048 to 1056); `framework/entity/AbstractAntEntity.java` `isSheltered` 1899 to 1904; `framework/entity/goal/ClipGrassGoal.java` line 82; `savanna/colony/RaidDefence.java` `brokenOpen` 137 to 151 and `planSeal` 164 to 189; `savanna/colony/Raid.java` `feedersOut` 1821 to 1854.
- **The proof.** The lines above read one after the other. `FleeGoal.canUse` returns false at line 67 when `colony.nest().isInside(...)` is true, before it asks whether danger is felt. The flight's goal (line 101) is any cell that `isInside` and is not a doorway. `isSheltered` is false in a feeding way. `Raid.inTheOpen` (lines 393 to 397) and the raiders pick their prey by `!isSheltered()`.
- **Unchecked link.** None for the rule. How many workers stand within `RetreatGoal`'s 4.5 blocks in a real raid was not measured.
- **The test.** `TermiteRaidDefenceTests.aWorkerAtABreaksMouthIsStungAndOneDeeperInIsLeft` and `theTermitesFeedingUnderTheMudStandInTheirGalleryWhenRaidersBreakIn` would both still pass with workers that never run.
- **The fix proposed.** One shared judge, "out of the hunters' reach", asked by the flight, the walk home and the hunters alike. The hunters' `isSheltered` is the right one, because it decides who is stung. `RetreatGoal`'s own list then shrinks to its real job (who packs the seal). The same single rule is the one to tune for BUGS 452 (termites nobody can get at), which is this finding's mirror.
- **Owner's call:** today workers in a raided gallery mostly stay and are taken. My pick: they run down the gallery to a sheltered cell as the real ones do; the raid's catch is then those caught before they get away, and soldiers.

## Q-3 (A, proven as code, a narrow case): a raid the player watches can reach a termite colony that is still only numbers, and then finds nobody

## Q-6 (B, proven as code): near and away disagree on what the termites remember of a raid

- **What is wrong.** The termites' memory of danger decides which holes stay shut and which are given up for good. A watched raid writes into it at every termite stung: each sting is a small real wound (half a heart of a worker's three), which counts as "a member hurt", 2 points, up to 12 at one place. An unwatched raid writes once, as one death (4 points), and only when the raid has got home; a raid that never gets home writes nothing.
- **What it costs.** After a watched raid of six or more stings the spot stays shut about three and a half days (12 points halving each day down to 1); after an unwatched one two days. A hole is given up for good after two attacks whose strength once reached 6: two watched raids do it whenever the second comes while the place is still remembered (four to five days), two unwatched ones only when the second comes within about a day of the first.
- **Where.** `savanna/colony/Raids.java` `bringHiddenHome` 579 to 596 (the one call at 591 to 594); `framework/fight/Fights.java` `strike` 129 to 133 (`STING_PRICK` 0.5, line 45); `framework/entity/AbstractAntEntity.java` `hurt` 715 to 722; `framework/colony/ColonyParts.java` line 105; `framework/colony/DangerMap.java` `hurt` 140 to 167, `add` 187 to 222, `isAttacked` 260 to 273, `hitHard` 279 to 290; `macrotermes.json` lines 27 to 44 and 266.

- **The fix proposed.** The unwatched raid tells the memory what it took at the time it takes it, one entry per termite, through the same call the bodies use (the catch is already counted in `RaidCatch.take`). One path, no special case in `bringHiddenHome`.

## Q-7 (B, proven as code): "a foe is near" and "the trouble is over" are each judged in five or more places with different distances and clocks

- **What is wrong.** No part can ask "is there trouble here, and is it over". Each worked out its own answer:

  - *The trouble is over:* the alarm fades 80 seconds after the last feeding; the seal opens at night, 30 seconds after the last foe within 16 blocks, or after a day whatever happens; a worker that fled stays in 60 seconds by its own clock (`hide_ticks` 1200); the holes stay shut by the danger memory's half-life; the raid itself ends its attack after 15 quiet seconds (`quiet_ticks` 300) or `fight_ticks`.
- **What it costs.** Every one of Q-1, Q-2, Q-4, Q-5, Q-6 and Q-8 is two such answers disagreeing. The next rule built here (the termites' side of BUGS 361, 452, 453) will need a sixth answer unless there is one to ask.
- **The fix proposed.** One small record per trouble kept by the alarm (where, what made it, when last felt, over or not) that the seal, the flight, the body carriers and the soldiers ask, and that a raid's end can close. `RaidDefence`'s breaches and the alarm's troubles are already nearly the same list (both: at most 8, a place, a last-foe time).

## Q-8 (B, proven as code; how often it bites is to measure): the raid reads the termites' alarm as "they have given this spot up"

- **What is wrong.** The raid asks the termite colony one question in three places: "do you keep off this spot?" So when the termites are alarmed at the spot before the attack starts, by the raid's own arrival, the raid is told "they keep off", and then: the judge at the halt says "no termite at the spot" (the column looks round, goes home, and the find is forgotten), or, if termites were seen and the attack does go ahead, the termites feeding under the mud are not brought out as bodies (the fix for BUGS 202 is skipped without a word in the diary) and the raiders going as numbers take nothing ("the termites keep off the spot").

- **Where.** `savanna/colony/SavannaParts.java` line 257 (the alarm answers `KEEP_OFF` with `keepsIn`); `framework/colony/Colony.java` `keepsOff` 216 to 222, whose comment says "for a lasting reason"; `savanna/colony/Raid.java` lines 378 (the judge), 1801 (`bringOutFeeders`, returns with no diary line) and 1936 (`takeHidden`); the same question in `savanna/colony/FeedingSpots.java` lines 90 and 99, which the feeders' cells and the soldiers' feeding beats are read from.

## Who has the termite

Minecraft lets a mob run one moving goal at a time; a goal with a smaller priority number pushes out a larger one; two goals with the same number never push each other out (the one that started first keeps the termite).

**A worker** (goal list: `savanna/entity/TermiteWorkerEntity.java` 92 to 129)

| Who wants it | When | What it does with it | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FleeGoal` (priority 0) | A hunter within 3 blocks, a stung nestmate within 3, or the alarm felt where it stands, and it is not "inside" by the nest's rule (Q-2) | Runs to the nearest cell the nest calls inside | Arrived, or 400 goal ticks; then it stays in 60 s by its own clock |
| `RetreatGoal` (priority 0) | The seal named it: it stood within about 4.5 blocks of a cover a Matabele bit open beside a tunnel | Walks deeper; the one nearest the plug stands just behind it | Arrived, or 300 goal ticks (30 s), or the seal is packed |
| The alarm (no order, only "no") | Its place is within a foe's or a break's reach | It may not go out, fetch food, scout, groom, nap or greet; it may still feed from inside, mend and carry | The trouble fades (Q-1: or never) |
| `SealGoal` (priority 2) | A leak in the nest's wall (the framework's rule, `NestWalls`) | Packs the hole; not stopped by the alarm, so the mender stands at the opening, where the hunters may take it (meant so: `isSheltered`'s comment) | Hole shut |
| `PlugHoleGoal` (priority 2) | A foraging hole wanted shut (day, or danger remembered there) | Plugs it | Hole shut |
| `CarryCorpseGoal` (priority 3) | A dead or paralysed nestmate, no foe near it by the refuse rule | Carries it in to be eaten (a paralysed one leaves the count the same way as one a raider takes) | Put down, or given up |
| The colony's crew rule (`Population.direct`) | More bodies than wanted, and it is out of sight | Turns it into a number | Never one that is stung, held, wounded, or brought out for a raid until the fight's end |
| The raid, the other colony's code | The attack starts and the colony is here | `Raid.feedersOut` turns termite numbers into bodies in the gallery cells (`comeOutAt`, `stayABodyUntil`) | The fight's longest time passes |
| The raid, as numbers | Raiders going as numbers | `RaidCatch.take` lowers the termites' count; unwatched, bodies in the open are taken straight away (`carriedOff`), never in front of a player | At once |
| A hunter | Its sting lands | The worker staggers about 2.5 s, then lies paralysed: every goal stops (it cannot move), it drops its load | A raider picks it up (`Raid.pickUp`), a nestmate carries it in, or it dies where it lies |

Can two take it at once? `FleeGoal` and `RetreatGoal` both sit at priority 0 and both move it; whichever starts first finishes, then the other may start. Both go inward, so nothing is lost; but a plugger that is fleeing first can be late, and the seal then waits its 10 seconds and packs without it. A stung worker can be wanted by a raider and by a nestmate at once; both check "still there, still paralysed" every tick and both end in the same `carriedOff`, so it is counted once (walked: `Raid.pickUp` 2163 to 2168, `CarryCorpseGoal.fetchParalysed` 210 to 219). Nothing found drops a load or a claim for good (a nestmate's claim on a paralysed body that it gives up because a foe came near is not handed back, but it runs out by itself: `Refuse.claimBody` 292 to 294).

## What is solid

- **A player walking up, standing, leaving.** Walking raises nothing. Sprinting or jumping on the mound (on a mound block, or within 3 blocks of the entrance) raises "something felt": minors within hearing come, look and drum; no worker is kept in; it is gone about 30 seconds after the last step. Sneaking raises nothing, and neither does a spectator (`Alarm.footsteps` line 312; a spectator is never on the ground, so the sprint look at line 345 passes it by too). A creative player counts like any other, which harms nothing. Walked: `framework/ModEvents.java` 103 to 139, `Alarm` 311 to 347. Tested: `TermiteAlarmTests.footstepsBringTheMinorsWithinEarshotToLookAndDrum`.
- **A player breaking in.** The break is a breach when the block is part of the mound or touches an open nest cell (`Alarm.isBreakIn` 286 to 308). Workers outside run in and stay in; soldiers within 16 blocks come and take their own places, the majors first; the mending is done by the framework's leak rule (`SealGoal` and `NestWalls.plugLeak`), which the alarm does not stop. That is a third mending rule beside the mound's own building (part A) and the raid seal, but the three do not fight: the leak rule fills an open hole in a wall, the seal is one planned plug in a tunnel, the mound's plan builds outward. A player who keeps the hole open keeps the breach alive (each break feeds it) and the menders keep coming; it ends 80 seconds after the last block broken. Only a player's break raises it (the event is the player's block-break event), so no animal can.
- **Nothing is walled in.** The raid seal waits while anything living stands in its cell and is forgotten after three give-up times (`RaidDefence.trySeal` 225 to 238). `ForagingHoles.plug` and `NestWalls.plugLeak` refuse while a stranger stands in the hole. `GalleryUpkeep.fillIn` fills only out of sight and never over a living thing or a hidden traveller. (A raider standing farther in than the hole being shut can still be walled in behind it: that is BUGS 361's pocket, and `MatabeleEntity.biteOut` is its way out.)
- **The seal is not dug open again by the diggers.** Its block is mound soil, which is not in the diggable tag, so `DigGoal` never takes it for a tunnel cell to dig (`Nest.isSoil` 776 to 778). And "a foe stuck by the break for ever" no longer holds the seal shut for ever (`RaidDefence.tick` 211 to 212).
- **Animals.** No savanna animal's code names an ant or termite (no match in `savanna/animal`), so animals do not hunt them; their steps and bulk raise no alarm (only players' steps do); the termites' animal foe list is empty. A vanilla monster seen by a forager is written into the danger memory as "seen", which for the termites closes nothing (`keep_away` false; holes close only where a member was hurt or killed). So a herd cannot keep a colony from foraging or building. Not checked: whether any vanilla mob damages termites by its own targeting.
- **Counts across the raid's seam.** A termite body taken by a raider leaves the count once (`carriedOff`, stands_for 1). A termite taken as a number leaves it once (`RaidCatch.take`). A stung termite wanted by both sides is counted once (see the table). The judge of "termites there now" (`Raid.termitesThere`) is one rule, asked before a raid is called and again as the front arrives (but see Q-8 for the one answer it should not listen to).
- **Reload.** Troubles are not saved and the colony is calm after a load (tested: `TermiteAlarmTests` line 322). A packed seal is saved and opens at night as before.
- **Peaceful, creative, spectator.** The mod has no check of its own for peaceful. The Matabele's sting on a player goes through `player.hurt` with a mob-attack source (`Fights.strike` line 140), which Minecraft scales by difficulty (MC `Player.java` line 936); I did not read the scaling function itself, so "no sting damage in peaceful" is likely, not proven. Creative players and spectators provoke nobody (`NestDefence` line 101). Termites attack no player at all (Q-11).
- **The cost of being ready (to measure, nothing alarming read).** A quiet termite colony that is here pays once a second for one look over every living thing in a box 128 by 49 by 128 blocks (`Alarm.tick` 348 to 350); soldiers and workers ask the alarm often but it answers at once while there is no trouble. In a trouble: once a second per trouble a walk over the nest's joined cells (`Alarm.cellsNear`), twice a second per breach a look for foes within 16 blocks. One thing outside the alarm to measure: `Nest.isInside` rebuilds its whole set of inside cells on the first ask of every tick (lines 1040 to 1081), and the workers' flight asks it every 5 ticks each, so with bodies about it is rebuilt every tick.

## Two troubles at once (what was walked)

- **A raid while a player digs.** Both are troubles in the same list; the worse kind within 8 blocks takes the other in (`Alarm.trouble` 216 to 249, `foldInto`). A breach stays where the wall was broken. Solid as read.
- **An alarm during the foraging night.** Workers within the reach stay in or run in; a hole closed for danger is plugged at once; the turns of which holes are open are picked only as a night starts (`HoleTurns.round` 100 to 110), so foragers out by a hole that closes come home by another. Solid as read.
- **A raid while builders have the mound's windows open (R5-5).** The raid's feeders are extra bodies that must stay bodies until the fight's end. Under the cap of 50 the crew rule then turns as many other workers, out of sight, into numbers (`Population.direct` 528 to 538). Whether a builder in the middle of a job is spared is R5-5's own question; this is one more way to reach it. Likely, not walked to the end.
- **A raid on a colony half numbers.** Q-3, Q-5, Q-6.
- **Not walked:** an alarm while a hidden trip is under way (`HiddenTrips`), a colony going away or coming back in the middle of a raid, a raid cut short by dusk, rain or a reload (the raid's own endings were read only as far as the attack and regroup).
