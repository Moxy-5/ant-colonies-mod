### The model workshop: from the user's inspiration picture to a model (the user, 2026-11-18; planned, nothing built yet)
The user makes an inspiration picture for every new animal and insect (front, side, back and top views on a plain background, plus a big posed picture). The Nile monitor's model took about 8 hours and two full restarts, so every new model follows this way of working.
- **Why it went slowly (read from the notes):** (1) detail before the basics were agreed: the first monitor was a full detailed, painted model at once ("like a normal lizard"), then patched heavier, blockier, rebuilt, started again; each time the detail sat on wrong proportions and was thrown away; the crocodile likewise had the wrong cube size twice (too fine, then too chunky). The third monitor went smoothly because it started with a flat outline from the picture, approved fast ("this outline is great"), then the 3D shape in plain colours, then detail, paint, animations. (2) Changes were told in words and Claude guessed; the model and the picture were never seen side by side at the same angle.
- **1. Picture in, one-page brief out (about 10 minutes).** The picture goes in `references/inspiration/<animal>.png`. A script cuts each view's outline out of the plain background and measures it (length against height and width, head length, how high the legs hold the body, the tail's share of the length) and takes the colours from the picture. Claude writes a brief: length in blocks, cube size (the pixel grid), the box budget, the key features. The user OKs it; "too chunky" or "too slim" is caught here, before any model.
- **2. Five stages, each with the user's OK:** outline (2D, drawn on top of the picture's own side, top and front views) -> 3D shape in plain colours -> shape detail -> paint -> animations. A change goes back to the stage it belongs to: "the head is too small" fixes the outline, never patches the painted model.
- **3. A Compare button in the model viewer:** the picture beside or behind the model at the same angle (side view by its side view, and so on), with a see-through slider. Claude checks this compare picture itself before showing the user, so fewer bad rounds reach the user.
- **4. Every version kept:** v1, v2, v3 switchable in the viewer, so "go back to v3's head" replaces starting over.
- **5. A parts library:** approved parts (the monitor's round legs, clawed feet, tail chain, jaw with teeth and mouth inside, eyes with lids; the crocodile's parts) become reusable parts with settings (length, thickness, toe count), so the next reptile starts from approved parts; part of the animal kit (M9, `docs/plans/animal-kit-tools-review.md`).
- **Tips for the user's pictures:** keep front, side, back and top on a plain background; write the wanted length in blocks (the crocodile picture said 5 to 6, the game's is 7.5); add one line on what matters most (for example "heavy and threatening, head held high").
- **Honest limits:** AI-made pictures often disagree between their views (leg count, head size), so measurements are a starting point, not exact; a monitor-sized model might take 2 to 3 hours instead of 8 (a guess, not measured). Building steps 1 to 4 is about one session; the parts library grows a little with each animal.
